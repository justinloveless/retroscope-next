# UF13 decisions: realtime and auth edge cases, load-robust timing tests

Spec: `docs/followups/UF13.md`. Amendments to U13, U14, U16, U17 and UF9 point back here.

## Contracts later units rely on

- **`IClientConnection.Holds(room)`** (U14 transport): true while the connection is open and holds the room (subscribed,
  not kicked or unsubscribed since). Every `IClientConnection` must implement it; the simulation's `SimConnection` does.
- **`IRoomPeer.Wanted`** (U13 actors, default `true`): the gateway's peer answers it from `Holds` and from whether the
  gateway still maps that peer. `RoomActor` does not start a subscription for a peer that is not wanted.
- **`ConnRateLimit.Take(borrow: true)`**: a frame that must not be refused may take its token on credit, up to one
  burst of debt.
- **`UsersRepo.SetAvatarAsync(user, image, onlyIfNone, now, ct)`**, **`UsersRepo.ClearAvatarAsync(user, now, ct)`** and
  **`BlobsRepo.InsertAvatar(...)`** (a batch command) replace the two-step insert-then-point. `OidcAccounts` now takes
  `UsersRepo` instead of `BlobsRepo`. `BlobsRepo.InsertAvatarAsync` remains for tests that need a bare avatar row.
- **`UsersRepo.VerifyAsync(user, proof, now, ct)`**: verifies an unverified row and writes its `auth.verify` audit in one
  statement; false when the row was already verified.

## Decisions

1. **Subscribe rate limit.** UF9 let the `sub` that lifts a `rate_limited` refusal through the limiter for free, so a
   client alternating refused commands and subs fetched one snapshot per sub, at any rate, until the abuse window
   closed the socket. That sub now borrows its token: it is let through while the connection is less than one burst
   in debt, and the debt is repaid before any other frame is allowed. Over any interval the connection is let through
   at most the refill plus two bursts. The UF9 rule "that sub is never dropped" becomes "never dropped unless the
   connection is already a burst in debt"; a client that backs off before resubscribing (as U28's does) never gets
   there, and one that does is still nacked `rate_limited` on its next command and resubscribes after its backoff.
   `LimitTests.After_a_rate_limited_nack_…` now waits 2.2 s after the sub instead of 1.2 s, because the sub costs a
   token.
2. **Kicked-connection subscribe.** A kick reaches the connection at once (`Connection.Kick` drops the room) but the
   actor's queue later. A `sub` the connection recorded before the kick, but whose subscribe the gateway handed over
   after it, used to start a subscription the connection no longer tracked: commands were nacked `not_subscribed`,
   and `Disconnected` never told the actor about the room. Two checks close it. The gateway's step drops (and
   forgets) a peer that is no longer wanted before posting, and `RoomActor.SubscribeAsync` drops a subscribe whose
   peer is no longer wanted when it reaches the front of the queue, which covers a kick, unsubscribe or close that
   happened while the subscribe was queued. The actor does not call `Leave` for such a peer, since a newer peer of
   the same connection may legitimately hold the subscription.
3. **Twins.** Two causes remained.
   - **Rejected `q`s were not remembered.** `_recentHashes` was set only for committed commands. A twin's rejected
     `q`, later passed by a commit, let the other twin's different intent under that `q` be acked `dup` without ever
     being decided (seed 11158 of 2001–12000). Every decided `q` now records its payload hash, so that intent is
     refused `cid_reuse` while the actor lives.
   - **The oracle judged twins as one tab.** In seed 1853 one twin's send of a copied intent got a final `internal`
     nack (a constraint rejection) and the other twin's later send of the same intent committed. Each answer was true
     for its own send. Intents copied into a live twin are now `Shared`: an ack in one twin after a final nack in the
     other is not judged against it. A second commit still fails.
   - **What stays is the residual §c.6 accepts.** "After a restart the LRU is empty and a dup is acked normally." When
     one twin's intent is decided under a `q` and the server restarts before the other twin sends a different intent
     under the same `q`, nothing durable tells them apart (seeds 292 and 4578). The oracle counts exactly that case as
     `twin intents lost to a restart (§c.6 residual)` and reports it in the run summary: the cid is held by twins,
     another intent was sent under the same `(room, cid, q)`, and every decision of it happened in an earlier server
     process. Any other `dup` for an undecided intent still fails, so the planted bug with the hash change reverted
     fails seed 11158 again.
   - Removing the residual needs durable memory per `q`: for example a window of the last committed `(q, hash)` pairs
     in `client_cursors` (a 0002 migration plus `CursorRepo` and `SqlRoomStore`), where a `q` at or below the cursor
     that is not in the window is decided again. Neither path is UF13's.
   - A client-side rule was tried and rejected: renumbering this page's own unsent entries whose `q` the cursor has
     passed fixed seed 292, but an entry created before the duplication is also held by the twin, which may have sent
     and committed it; renumbering it applied it twice (seed 4243).
   - `Twins` is in `Faults.Default` (which now equals `Faults.All`).
4. **Avatar upload.** `UsersRepo.SetAvatarAsync` runs the blob insert and the pointer update (which deletes the avatar
   it replaced, using PostgreSQL 18's `old.avatar_blob_id`) as one batch in one transaction, and rolls back when no
   user row matches or, for the Google profile picture (`onlyIfNone`), the user already has an avatar. Before, the
   upload and `OidcAccounts.SetAvatarIfNoneAsync` each inserted the blob in its own statement, so a failure or crash
   before the second statement left a blob nothing referenced (avatars are not garbage collected).
5. **Magic-link verify.** The session holder's link verified only when the cached session said the account was
   unverified. The cache can be stale (the row changed without an eviction), so a verify could be skipped or
   audited twice. The decision is now the `email_verified_at is null` condition of the update itself, in one statement
   with its audit row. The cached principals are evicted only when the row changed.
6. **Load-robust timing tests.**
   - **Markup and ADF** (`MarkupJiraLengthTimingTests`, `AdfCapsTests.Hostile_shapes_convert_quickly`): each hostile
     shape is timed at a quarter of its size and at full size, seven interleaved runs each, fastest run counted, short
     operations repeated to at least 2 ms per sample (`tests/Retroscope.Core.Tests/Timing/Scaling.cs`). Linear work
     measured 3.4–5.2x, and the past-the-node-cap ADF shape 1.9x. The limit is 8x. A planted quadratic bracket scan
     (a vectorized scan to the end of the text from every `[`, much cheaper than the old `ScanLink` walk) failed the
     bracket-heavy rows at 8.5–13x, and a planted quadratic island count failed the wide ADF shape at 11x. The
     200 ms and 2 s budgets are gone.
   - **Login parity**: nine alternating wrong-password and unknown-email logins, each from its own /24 so the
     per-account backoff never answers 429 instead of verifying, comparing the fastest of each kind (still `> 0.6x`).
     With `Bcrypt.DummyVerify` removed, it failed with 5.4 ms against 77.2 ms.
   - **Realtime**: `ActorHarness.Wait` and the transport's `Wait.Default` are failure detectors only (a passing test
     never waits them out), so they grow from 10 s to 60 s. The keep-alive timeout in `LivenessTests` grows from
     300 ms to 1.5 s and the silent client waits out two timeouts plus five intervals. The hello timeout in
     `HandshakeTests` grows from 300 ms to 1.5 s and the test waits two of them. The welcome clock check allows a
     minute instead of 5 s.
   - Test placement: the new tests live in the folders whose categories the Check runs (`Actors`, `Transport`,
     `AuthCore`, `Email`). `Scaling.cs` is new, used only by the two timing tests above.

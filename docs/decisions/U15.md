# U15 decisions: simulation harness

U15 owns `tests/Retroscope.Sim/**`.

## What runs

`dotnet run --project tests/Retroscope.Sim -- --seeds 2000` (the Check, and the `simulation (PR seeds)` step of
`./build.sh test`) runs seeds 1–2000, 200 steps each, in parallel, then the teeth check. One seed is one world:

- **The real server code**: `RoomActor` from `Server/Realtime/Actors` (started through a real `ActorRegistry`), the
  real `PokerDomain` and `TeamDecider`, the real `Policy.Can` and the real projections. The bindings follow the U21
  sketch in U13's follow-ups (`PokerInput` conversion, `DurabilityOf`, `Supersedes`, `WarmUntil`, `OnShutdown`).
- **A poker session and its team**, cross-posting as §c.2 says: a reveal posts `PokerChanged` to the team; a
  membership change (an HTTP ask to the team actor) refreshes the membership cache and posts `RosterChanged` and
  `AccessChanged`; a simulated board creates action items in place, edits and deletes their cards.
- **Five tabs of four people** (one person with two tabs). Each follows the client rules of §c.3, §g.4 and U28
  (`outbox.js`, `room.js`): same `(cid, q)` after a reconnect, renumber after a retryable nack and for unsent entries
  at sync, acks deferred while resyncing, `cid_reuse` → re-mint and renumber, 4409 → re-mint keeping `q`s, ephemeral
  kinds dropped after 10 s, kicked rooms dropped. Poker frames fold into U7's `ViewerModel` (linked from
  `Retroscope.Core.Tests`, so the sim and the golden transcripts use one reference fold); team frames fold into
  `TeamViewModel`.
- **Faults** (`--faults default`): network drops (frames in flight lost), commit faults per room (broken before
  COMMIT, outcome unknown with and without the commit landing, constraint rejection, fencing loss, crash after
  COMMIT before the ack), `kill -9`, graceful redeploy (actors drain, then bye), client storage faults (intact
  reload, regressed `cseq`, lost outbox, lost storage, a duplicated tab whose original closes), membership changes,
  board posts, voting bursts (auto-reveal), and both actors stalled with full external queues while they keep posting
  to each other.

### Invariants (§j)

| Invariant | How it is checked |
|---|---|
| No double application | The store counts commits per intent (every resend, renumbering and copy of an outbox entry is one intent); more than one fails. |
| No lost acked command | A non-dup ack whose last decision had committed events requires the commit; after healing, the live rooms' projections equal a reload from the database (`Db.LoadPoker`/`LoadTeam`). |
| Acks tell the truth | A committed intent answered with a final nack fails. A `dup` ack means "this `q` was processed", so it is accepted for an intent the server decided (a rejection whose nack was lost); an intent the server never decided at all and that is acked `dup` was skipped: lost. |
| No unacked intent lost to a transient failure | After healing every intent is acked, nacked, dropped by a client rule, or lost to an injected storage fault. |
| No stale overwrite accepted | At commit, the store compares `setTicket.baseVersion`, `settings.expected`, `next.fromRoundId` and reorder permutations with the rows before the commit. |
| Every client's final view equals a fresh snapshot | Each tab's fold, presence included, is serialized and compared byte for byte with `RoomView.SnapFrameAsync` for that viewer. |
| Seq gapless per epoch per viewer | A tx or replay that skips a seq fails (the simulated network never drops single frames). |
| Presence grace | A `presence.leave` fails if the principal had a server-side subscription to the room within the grace before it. |
| Auto-reveal at most once per generation | A round revealed twice without a replay in between fails (the generation itself is memory only). |
| Secrecy | A tx `poker.vote.state` with another principal's points, or a snapshot with points of a voting round, fails. |
| No deadlock | Every wait on an actor has a 20 s real-time limit. |

### Teeth

After the 2000 seeds pass, the run plants each bug and searches the same seeds until one fails:

- **Cursor** (`--inject cursor`): the persisted cursor is read one lower, which is how an actor checking `q < cursor`
  instead of `q <= cursor` behaves. Caught in about 65 % of seeds (a resend after an unknown commit outcome is
  applied again: double application, or a committed intent answered with a final nack).
- **Precondition** (`--inject precondition`): the poker decider sees `setTicket`/`settings.update` with the server's
  current values. Caught in about 44 % of seeds (stale overwrite accepted).

### Other entry points

`--seed S --trace` replays one seed with a step-by-step trace; `--seeds 50000` is the nightly run (about 5 minutes on 12 cores, extrapolated from the 10,000-seed run);
`--determinism` runs every seed twice and compares a digest of the outcome; `--strict` also fails on known open issues;
`--faults all` (or `Default,StaleStorage,Twins,StaleSocket`) adds the opt-in faults below.

## Decisions where the spec was silent or did not fit

1. **A simulation clock of its own instead of `FakeTimeProvider`.** `FakeTimeProvider.Advance` fires every due timer
   in one call, with the clock already moved, while actors run on the thread pool, so what an actor reads as "now"
   depends on thread timing. `SimClock` fires one timer at a time, at its own due time, and the world waits for quiet
   before the next. The `Microsoft.Extensions.TimeProvider.Testing` reference is gone from the project.
2. **Quiet is read from the actors' fields.** The runtime offers no "idle" signal, so the world reads, by reflection,
   each actor's two private channels (`TryPeek` on the single-reader internal one, which cannot count; `Count` on the
   bounded external one) and the two pending reads of `RoomActor.NextAsync`: an actor is parked when both channels
   are empty and both reads are still pending (a read that completed means it woke). Parked must hold across three
   looks a yield apart, because a wake-up travels through the thread pool. Nothing is enqueued to find out: a barrier
   message would itself change behaviour (an idle actor does not retire while anything is queued, and any message
   reloads a poisoned actor).
3. **One enqueue at a time.** Two actors running at once can interleave their frames on a shared socket, so the world
   enqueues one message, waits for quiet, and only then the next. Actor-to-actor posts are made on the posting actor's
   thread and enqueued by the world once everything is quiet (still non-blocking, §c.2); the poster always finishes
   its message first. A graceful shutdown drains the actors first and then says bye to every socket, as U14
   decision 5 orders it. A crash is signalled before the process is marked dead, so the world never acts on a dead
   process without knowing it crashed. Three `--determinism` sweeps over seeds 1–4000 replayed every seed identically.
4. **Sockets.** A socket whose client vanished still takes writes until the server notices (frames are observed and
   dropped), as a real one does until keep-alive gives up.
5. **Storage faults follow U28's layout.** `cseq` and the entries are one stored value, so "regressed `cseq`" loses the
   entries newer than the older `cseq` with it. Replacing all of sessionStorage with an older copy (whose unsent
   entries may since have been sent) is the opt-in `StaleStorage` fault, because U28 renumbers such entries at sync.
6. **Constraint rejections only hit client commands.** A rejected internal transition (a spotlight settle, a text sync)
   leaves the live state ahead of or behind the rows until the next reload; that is a SQL bug's consequence, not a
   protocol property.
7. **Rooms simulated: poker and team.** They carry every Check item (outcome unknown, storage faults, full queues
   cross-posting, spotlight settle loss, preconditions). A retro room (anonymous tags, readiness, the timer's
   `expired` once) is a follow-up.
8. **`heldByThisTab`** is taken from the server's overlay when comparing views, as U7's property tests do: it cannot be
   folded from tx frames.

## What the stores must do (for U21 and U23)

The in-memory stores model the SQL the simulation depends on; each rule below was found by a failing seed:

- **U21:** `SpotlightSettled` writes `spotlight_round_id` and `spotlight_client_id` from the event. `PokerApply` only
  clears `Dirty`, because the live state already has the position from the ephemeral moves.
- **U23:** loading the team room refreshes the membership cache and posts `RosterChanged` and `AccessChanged` to the
  poker (and board) rooms. A member change whose commit outcome was unknown landed without its fan-out; the HTTP retry
  is then a no-op, so nothing else would ever update the live poker roster or kick a removed member.
- **U23:** team writes are column-scoped and never resurrect a row; text sync is guarded by `source_version < $v`;
  the load-time repair query brings copies up to the card's version.

## Open issues found (other units)

| Name | Faults | What happens | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| `lost-retryable-nack` (known; reported on every run, fails with `--strict`) | default (full queues + drop or crash) | A command is refused `server_busy` at enqueue; the client, not having read the nack yet, sends a later command of the same room, which commits; the socket dies before the nack is read. The resend of the refused `q` is below the cursor and is acked `dup`: the intent is silently lost. 9 of seeds 1–2000 (first: 819); 50 of seeds 2001–12000. | U13 or U14, with U28 |
| 4409 keeps `q`s | `StaleSocket`, `Twins` | A tab reconnects while the server still holds a socket bound to its cid (its own dead socket until keep-alive gives up, or a duplicated tab): 4409, a new cid, the outbox resent with the same `q`s under it. Entries that committed under the old cid but whose acks were lost are decided again: a final nack for a committed intent, or a second commit. Seeds 51 (`StaleSocket`) and 13 (`Twins`). | U28 (U14) |
| Renumbering restored entries | `StaleStorage` | An entry restored from an older copy of sessionStorage, unsent there but sent and committed since, is renumbered at sync (U28 decision 2) and applied again. Seed 697. | U28 |

The `cid_reuse` path itself is reached about once in 2000 seeds even with `Twins`: 4409 keeps two live tabs from
sharing a cid, as §c.6 expects. One hazard there was seen only through a storage state U28 cannot produce, so it is a
note, not a finding: when a `cid_reuse` nack for `q=6` arrives, a `q=7` pipelined behind it may already have
committed under the old cid, and renumbering every entry would apply it again.

Suggested fixes, for the owners to weigh:

- `lost-retryable-nack`: the actor could refuse every later command of a cid on that connection once one was refused
  `server_busy`, or close the socket (1012) instead of nacking, so the in-order resend after reconnect covers the
  refused command; or `client_cursors` could record the `q`s refused below the cursor until the next commit, so a
  resend of one is decided instead of acked `dup`. `rate_limited` from the transport has the same shape.
- 4409: before re-minting, the client could resend its sent entries once under the old cid when a socket for it is
  accepted again (or learn the old cid's cursor), so committed entries come back `dup`.
- Restored entries: mark entries loaded from storage as possibly sent, so sync keeps their `q` and the cursor dedupes
  them.

## Verification

- `dotnet run --project tests/Retroscope.Sim -- --seeds 2000`: 2000/2000 seeds pass (about 11 s on 12 cores), with
  214,643 commands, 10,184 commit faults, 4,333 crashes (kill, fencing, crash after commit), 1,918 redeploys, 8,649
  drops, 5,993 storage faults, 2,077 full-queue episodes, 120 unsettled spotlight positions lost in crashes and
  1,295 auto-reveals; the known `lost-retryable-nack` issue is reported for 9 seeds; both planted bugs are caught
  (cursor at seed 1, precondition at seed 5).
- `--seeds 10000 --start 2001`: all pass (the known issue in 50 seeds).
- `--inject cursor --seeds 1000` fails 655 seeds; `--inject precondition --seeds 1000` fails 447.
- `--determinism --seeds 4000`, three times: every seed replays identically.

## Amendment (UF9)

1. **Through the real gateway.** Simulated sockets are `IClientConnection`s and every `sub`, `cmd` and close goes
   through `RoomGateway`. Quiet is read from `IQuiescent.IsParked`, not by reflection.
2. **Open issues closed.** `lost-retryable-nack`, "4409 keeps `q`s" and "renumbering restored entries" are fixed (UF9
   decisions 1–4). `StaleStorage` and `StaleSocket` are default fault classes, `build.sh` runs `--strict`, and no
   known issue remains. `Twins` stays opt-in (UF9 decision 9: 2 of seeds 1–2000).
3. **A board.** A team-visible board with stages, enforced readiness, anonymous posts and a short shared timer joins
   the world, with the oracle checks of UF9 decision 9. The run summary counts board timer expiries.
4. **Verification (UF9).** `--seeds 2000 --strict`: 2000/2000 pass, 215,219 commands, 10,115 commit faults, 4,352
   crashes, 1,989 redeploys, 2,042 board timer expiries; both planted bugs are caught. Seeds 819, `51 --faults
   Default,StaleSocket`, `13 --faults Default,Twins` and `697 --faults Default,StaleStorage` pass with `--strict`;
   `--determinism --seeds 300` replays identically. Planting an unpersisted timer expiry fails 199 of 300 seeds and a
   leaked anonymous author 77 of 100.

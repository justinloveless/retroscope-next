# UX15 decisions: a volume slider for the retro timer

The owner asked for the retro timer's volume to be a regular slider rather than a menu of four levels
(`Volume 25/50/75/100%`, U35 decision 7), on a logarithmic curve so the useful range is not bunched at one end.

UX15 adds `web/src/retro/volume.js`, `tests/web-unit/retro/volume.test.js` and this file. It edits
`web/src/retro/{timer,track}.js`, `web/styles/{kit,retro}.css`, `tests/web-unit/retro/track.test.js`, the fake
e2e specs `retro-stages/{_stages,timer}.spec.js` (the fake AudioContext's params gain `setTargetAtTime`) and the
screenshot capture list.

## Decisions

1. **A level from 0 to 100 on a 40 dB curve.** `volume.js` is pure: `gain(level)` is
   `10 ** ((level / 100 − 1) × 40 / 20)`, so 100 is a gain of 1, 75 is 0.316, 50 is 0.1, and every 10 steps
   is the same ratio (about 4 dB), which the ear hears as an even change. With a linear gain, everything from 50 %
   to 100 % sounds nearly alike and the audible change sits in the bottom fifth. 0 is silence, not −40 dB, so the
   bottom of the slider means off. 40 dB was chosen over 60: the timer plays through laptop speakers next to a
   meeting, where the bottom of a 60 dB range is inaudible, so a third of the slider would do nothing.
2. **The browser keeps the level; everything else still multiplies by the gain.** The new key is
   `localStorage['rs.timer.level']`. `sound.volume` stays the linear gain, now derived from the level, so the
   alarm, the generated music and the track (UX13) are unchanged. The gain is also still written to
   `rs.timer.volume`, so a tab still on the previous bundle plays at the same loudness. At level 0 the value
   written is 0.0001 (`legacyGain`), not 0: the previous bundle reads 0 as unset and would play at its 0.5
   default, while 0.0001 is 80 dB down, so that tab stays near-silent. A level migrated from 0.0001 is 0.
3. **Migration.** With no level stored, the level is the one that sounds as loud as the stored gain:
   `100 × (1 + 20 log10(g) / 40)`, rounded and clamped. The old 0.5 default is level 85, which is also the new
   default, so nobody's timer changes loudness: 0.25 becomes 70, 0.75 becomes 94 and 1 stays 100. A missing,
   unparsable or non-positive gain gives the default, and any stored level is clamped to 0..100.
4. **A popover replaces the menu.** The sound button opens `createPopover({ role: 'dialog', label: 'Timer sound' })`,
   the same pattern as the story-points popover. It has two groups:
   - "Everyone on the board", for facilitators only: the alarm and music flags, as switches, sending the same
     `retro.timer.setFlags` the menu sent. They were check-or-circle menu rows, which read as radio choices.
   - "This browser": a Mute switch and the Volume slider with its percentage.

   Each group is `role="group"`, labelled by its heading through `aria-labelledby`, so a screen reader announces
   the heading when focus enters a group. The headings say which settings are shared, which the menu never did. Opening the popover focuses the
   slider, the control people open it for; Escape closes it and returns focus to the button.
5. **A native `<input type=range>`.** U34 decision 2 and U35 decision 7 avoided native form controls in WebKit
   because `<select>` reports a `style-src-attr` violation under the production CSP. A range input was never
   tested, so UX15 tested it. A throwaway fake e2e spec created a `<select>`, a bare range and a range with the
   app's classes, then focused, keyed and dragged each, in chromium, firefox, webkit and mobile-webkit. The
   `<select>` reported `style-src-attr` in webkit and mobile-webkit, which proves the probe catches the
   violation. Neither range reported anything in any engine. The timer spec now drives the styled slider in all
   four projects, and the harness fails any test that reports a violation, so a regression would show there. A
   native range brings keyboard support, touch dragging and the `slider` role for free, so no custom
   `ui/slider.js` was written.
6. **Drawn in the switch's colours.** `.range` in `kit.css` uses `appearance: none`, a 6 px track in
   `hsl(var(--input))`, a filled part and a ringed thumb in `hsl(var(--primary))`, and the `.input` focus ring
   (2 px `--ring`) on the thumb. WebKit has no pseudo-element for the filled part, so the track is a gradient
   split at `--range-fill`, which script sets through the CSSOM (`setStyle`), not a style attribute. Firefox
   draws the fill itself (`::-moz-range-progress`). While muted, the fill and thumb turn `--muted-foreground`, so
   the kept level stays visible but reads as inactive.
7. **Mute keeps the level; moving the slider unmutes.** Picking a volume in the menu unmuted, and the slider does
   the same on its first step. Level 0 counts as silent, like mute: the sound button shows `volume-x`, and
   neither the music nor the alarm plays. The Mute switch stays off at level 0, because it reflects the stored
   mute flag only. The accessible value is `aria-valuetext` "60%", or "Off" at 0. The visible label
   and percentage are `aria-hidden`, because the slider already carries both.
8. **Live on input, kept on change.** Each `input` event sets the level and gain at once without writing
   storage. `change` (release, or any key) writes the level, the gain and the mute flag. `paint()` updates the
   switches and the slider in place and sets the slider's value only when it differs, so a slider being
   dragged is never rebuilt or moved under the pointer.
9. **Gain changes glide.** Assigning `gain.value` while sound plays steps the waveform and clicks ("zipper
   noise" when dragging). The generated music's output gain and the track's gain now start at their level when
   created and, after that, move with `setTargetAtTime(target, currentTime, 0.015)` only when the target
   changes. The old code assigned the generated music's gain on every 250 ms tick; it now touches the param only
   on a change. The track unit test's fake param records glides, and it still asserts the level at each volume.
10. **The first press on the sound button is no longer lost.** While audio is locked, the first `pointerdown`
    unlocks it, and the repaint replaced the button's icon, the element the press landed on, so Chromium and
    WebKit dropped the click: the menu, and now the popover, did not open on that first press. The icon is now
    replaced only when it changes, and the button's `svg` has `pointer-events: none`, so the press lands on the
    button itself. A timer spec installs the fake AudioContext, which starts suspended and resumes at once,
    checks that the button shows `triangle-alert` while locked, and checks that one click opens the popover.
11. **The popover closes when the timer goes idle.** The sound button lives in the running timer's controls,
    which hide when the timer stops. An open popover would then stay on screen, anchored to a hidden button,
    and the next reposition would place it at the viewport margin (8, 8). `update()` closes it whenever the
    timer is idle.

## Verification

- `node --test tests/web-unit/retro/volume.test.js tests/web-unit/retro/track.test.js`: 27 of 27 passed. The
  volume tests cover the curve, equal ratios, monotonic gain, the default, each old menu volume's migration,
  invalid values, the legacy gain written at level 0, clamping, stored versus legacy keys, the text, and the
  glide call.
- `./build.sh quick`: passed (49 web unit tests, including the 16 track tests with the glide check).
- `./build.sh fake-e2e "retro-stages/timer.spec.js"`: 52 of 52 passed in chromium, firefox, webkit and
  mobile-webkit. With the idle close and the level-0 icon reverted, the new checks failed in all four. The new
  timer specs cover:
  - the default level;
  - a live gain change on playing music (0.08 × 0.1 at level 50);
  - storage on change;
  - mute keeping the level;
  - the slider unmuting;
  - level 0 stopping the music, showing `volume-x` and storing a legacy gain of 0.0001;
  - a reload keeping the level;
  - the keys ArrowLeft, ArrowRight, Home ("Off") and End;
  - Escape returning focus;
  - a mouse drag that leaves the same slider element in place;
  - migration from `rs.timer.volume = 0.25` to 70;
  - the facilitator flags in their labelled group, and their absence for a guest;
  - the popover closing when the timer goes idle;
  - the locked-audio warning icon, and one click unlocking and opening the popover.

- An earlier run of `./build.sh fake-e2e "retro-stages/timer*.spec.js"`, before the review fixes, passed 59
  of 60. The one failure is `timer-track.spec.js` "two tabs play the track at the shared position" in webkit
  or mobile-webkit. It fails the same way on `main` (d6d1a58, two runs): headless WebKit's element advanced 0.34 s
  in 1 s. That is UX13's known WebKit gap, and that spec never changes the volume.
- Screenshots `retro-timer-sound-{light,dark}-{desktop,mobile}` (`capture.spec.js`, state `timer-sound`) were
  checked by eye.

## Open

- The popover does not trap focus. Tab from the slider leaves it, as it does in every other popover in the app.
- Volume is not synchronised between tabs of the same browser until they reload, as before.
